MasterClass — best for learning from famous chefs

MasterClass sells access: annual membership, on-demand video lessons taught by household-name chefs and food figures, beautifully produced. You're not just learning a recipe — you're watching how a great cook thinks. The cooking and baking catalog is deep enough to keep you busy for months.

The honest catch: it's inspiration-first, not curriculum-first. There's no instructor checking your knife cuts, no quizzes, no progression path — you're on your own for practice and feedback. And it's a membership, not per-class: great if you binge, wasteful if you watch two videos and forget it.

Who it's for: self-directed learners who get motivated by watching masters work.

Who should skip it: anyone who needs structure, assignments, or feedback to actually improve.

Udemy cooking courses — best for one cheap skill

Udemy is a marketplace: thousands of individual cooking and baking courses from independent instructors, bought per-course, yours to keep. Want just sourdough? Just knife skills? Just French pastry? Buy that one course — often at a steep sale price — and ignore the rest of the catalog.

The honest catch: quality varies wildly because anyone can publish. Read the reviews and check the instructor's credentials before buying — a 4.8 with 2,000 ratings means something; a 5.0 with 6 ratings means nothing. List prices are theater; the sale price is the real price.

Who it's for: learners who want one specific skill without a subscription.

Who should skip it: anyone who wants a coherent curriculum instead of hunting through a marketplace.

Sur La Table online classes — best live instruction

Sur La Table runs real classes over live video — typically 90 minutes to 2 hours, per household, with a prep packet (ingredient and equipment lists) sent ahead. You cook along in your own kitchen while an instructor teaches and answers questions in real time. It's the closest thing to standing in their store classroom.

The honest catch: it's priced per class, per household — a few sessions cost more than a year of an on-demand membership. You're also on their schedule, not yours, and you'll need your ingredients bought and prepped before the call.

Who it's for: cooks who learn by doing alongside a teacher and asking questions live.

Who should skip it: night-shift or unpredictable-schedule learners who need on-demand everything.

America's Test Kitchen Online Cooking School — best real curriculum

ATK's cooking school is the only one here built like actual school: structured courses across techniques, recipes, and fundamentals, taught by the test-kitchen cooks who develop the recipes — the people who make all the mistakes so you don't have to. Quizzes and progression included. If you want to understand why the recipe works, this is the one.

The honest catch: it's the most “school” of the four, which means it demands the most of you. No celebrity sparkle, no cook-along party energy — it's methodical. Membership pricing rewards the committed and punishes the dabbler.

Who it's for: serious home cooks who want technique fundamentals, not just recipes.

Who should skip it: anyone shopping for entertainment or a single fun date-night class.

Buyer questions, answered

Live or on-demand — which is better?
Live (Sur La Table) gives you feedback and accountability; on-demand (the other three) gives you schedule freedom. Beginners improve faster with feedback; busy people finish more with on-demand.

Do online cooking classes actually work?
For technique, yes — if you cook along instead of just watching. Watching a knife-skills video without a knife in your hand is entertainment, not education.

What's the cheapest way to start?
A single Udemy course on sale is the lowest entry price of the four. Just verify the instructor and the rating count first.
