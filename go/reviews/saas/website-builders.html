Social platforms rent you an audience; your website is the one you own. But most creators don't want a second job as a webmaster — the right builder is the one you'll still update six months from now.

We ranked these four for creators: design quality out of the box, how fast you can publish, and whether the platform grows with you.

The lineup

Squarespace — Best design out of the box
Squarespace's whole pitch is that your site looks expensive without hiring anyone. For photographers, writers, and personal brands, the templates do most of the design work for you.
Best for: Creators whose site is a portfolio or personal brand.
The catch: You trade flexibility for polish — very custom layouts fight the system.
Check plans & pricing →
Webflow — Best for designers
Webflow is the builder for people who care about every pixel — visual building with the kind of control that used to require code. Designers love it; beginners should budget learning time.
Best for: Creators (or their hired help) who want a truly custom site.
The catch: This is not a weekend project for a total beginner. The power comes with a learning curve.
Check plans & pricing →
Wix — Easiest to start
Wix is the path of least resistance: pick a template, drag things around, publish today. It won't win design awards, but it gets you on the web fast.
Best for: Creators who want a site up this week with minimum fuss.
The catch: Easy in, harder out — moving a Wix site elsewhere later is a rebuild.
Check plans & pricing →
Framer — Best for landing pages
Framer started as a prototyping tool and grew into a site builder — which shows in the best way for landing pages. If your site is really a series of launches, it's worth a look.
Best for: Creators running launches, campaigns, and link-in-bio-style pages.
The catch: It's strongest at pages, weaker at sprawling blogs or stores.

Buyer questions, answered

Can I switch builders later?
Yes, but it's a rebuild — content moves, design doesn't. Pick assuming you'll stay a few years.

Do I need hosting separately?
No — all four include hosting. You only need separate hosting if you go the WordPress route.

Which is best for selling products?
Squarespace and Wix both include commerce; Webflow handles custom stores. For serious selling, compare their transaction fees on the pricing page.
