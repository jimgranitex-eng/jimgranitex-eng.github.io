Why these picks

  • Daily practice beats study marathons — all four apps are built around short, repeatable sessions.
  • Each method is different: game-style streaks, structured lessons, immersive imagery, or pure audio. Match the method to your day.
  • Free starts exist — Duolingo's free tier and published trials from the others let you test the method before paying.

The honest catch

The honest limitation: no app alone makes you fluent. Apps are strongest as daily practice between real-world exposure — media, classes, or conversation partners. Anyone promising fluency from an app alone is selling, not teaching.

Who this is for

  • Beginners who want a daily practice habit with zero upfront cost
  • Commuters who can study by audio for 30 minutes a day
  • Travelers who want conversational basics before a trip

Who should skip it

  • Advanced learners who need conversation practice with real people — an app can't replace that
  • Anyone who won't open the app daily — streak mechanics only work if you play along

Questions, answered

Can an app make me fluent?
No app alone can promise fluency — and we're honest about that. Apps build vocabulary, grammar, and daily habits. Fluency needs real conversation and exposure on top.

Which is best for beginners?
Duolingo for a free start and daily habit; Babbel if you want structured lessons aimed at conversation from day one.

How much time does it take per day?
Most of these apps are designed around 10–30 minute daily sessions — Pimsleur's lessons run about 30 minutes by design.
