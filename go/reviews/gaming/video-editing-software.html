Adobe Premiere Pro — best for going pro

Premiere Pro is the industry-standard timeline editor: the one most professional editors, studios, and collaborators already use. Deep toolset, huge tutorial ecosystem, tight integration with After Effects and the rest of Adobe's suite. If you ever hand a project to an editor — or become one — this is the common language.

The honest catch: subscription-only, forever — stop paying, stop editing. It's also the steepest learning curve here; the depth that pros love is exactly what overwhelms beginners. And it wants a capable machine.

Who it's for: creators building a real editing practice or collaborating with working editors.

Who should skip it: casual cutters — you're renting a race car to drive to the store.

DaVinci Resolve — best free pro editor

DaVinci Resolve's free tier is the best deal in editing software: a genuinely professional editor — legendary color grading included — for nothing. Multi-track timeline, Fusion effects, Fairlight audio: the free version alone outguns most paid consumer editors. The paid Studio version exists for high-end workflows most creators never touch.

The honest catch: free doesn't mean easy — Resolve's interface assumes you want to learn editing properly, and it shows. It's also heavier on hardware than the consumer editors; older laptops will feel it.

Who it's for: creators who want pro-grade tools without a subscription.

Who should skip it: anyone who wants templates and one-click results over a real toolset.

Filmora — best for beginners

Filmora (by Wondershare) is built for the first-time editor: drag-and-drop timeline, big template and effects library, AI helpers, and an interface that doesn't require a tutorial to open. Shorts, gameplay highlights, cooking videos — the stuff creators actually make — come together fast.

The honest catch: ease has a ceiling. Complex projects, serious color work, and pro collaboration workflows will push you toward Premiere or Resolve eventually. Watch the pricing model on the merchant page — subscription vs. perpetual terms matter here.

Who it's for: beginners and casual creators who want finished videos today, not a semester of learning.

Who should skip it: anyone already editing seriously — you'll hit the ceiling quickly.

CapCut Commerce Pro — best for fast product & short-form video

CapCut's Commerce Pro is the AI-assisted route: built for turning product clips and short-form ideas into finished videos fast — templates, AI editing suggestions, and performance analytics aimed at selling, not just posting. If your videos exist to move product (affiliate or otherwise), it's purpose-built for that job, with a free tier to start.

The honest catch: it's optimized for commerce-style short video, not long-form storytelling — don't buy it expecting a Premiere replacement. AI-generated output still needs your taste; the tool is fast, not magic.

Who it's for: creators making product videos and shorts where speed and volume matter.

Who should skip it: long-form editors and anyone who wants full manual control of every cut.

Buyer questions, answered

Free vs. paid — where's the real line?
DaVinci Resolve's free tier covers genuine pro work; CapCut's free tier covers fast short-form. You pay when you need collaboration, the deepest toolsets (Premiere), or the easiest on-ramp (Filmora).

Which one for YouTube Shorts?
All four cut vertical video. For pure speed on shorts, CapCut Commerce Pro and Filmora are fastest to learn; for a craft you're building on, Resolve (free) or Premiere.

Will my computer run these?
Check each merchant's system requirements before committing — Premiere and Resolve are the most demanding, Filmora and CapCut the lightest. Editing 4K wants real hardware no matter the editor.
