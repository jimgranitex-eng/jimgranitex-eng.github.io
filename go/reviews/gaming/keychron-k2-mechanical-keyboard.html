Why this one

  • The 75% layout keeps arrows and function keys while dropping the numpad — the layout most buyers actually want once they try it, per owner consensus.
  • Wireless (Bluetooth, 3 devices) plus wired USB-C: desk workers pair it with laptop/tablet/phone and switch with a keystroke.
  • Hot-swappable versions let you change switches without soldering — the upgrade path that turns a $90 board into a hobby.

The honest catch

Keycaps are ABS (shine with heavy use — PBT versions exist at higher tiers). And like all mechanical boards, it's louder than a membrane keyboard; roommates have opinions.

Who should buy it

  • First mechanical keyboard buyers
  • Mac + Windows switchers (dedicated OS toggle)
  • Desk setup builders wanting wireless flexibility

Who should skip it

  • Num pad essential workers (get a full-size board)
  • Silent-office workers (consider low-profile or silent switches)
  • Hardcore custom hobbyists (you'll outgrow it — that's fine)

Buyer questions, answered

Hot-swappable or not?
Both versions exist — check the listing. Hot-swap costs a bit more and is worth it if you might change switches.

Which switches?
Brown for most buyers (tactile, not clicky). Red for gaming/quiet. Blue only if you live alone.

Does it work with Mac?
Yes — includes Mac keycaps and an OS toggle switch. One of its headline features.
